## Claims in the voiceover

| # | Claim (as spoken / shown) | Status | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Most airliner cabin windows have a tiny hole near the bottom, on purpose. | ✅ Confirmed – usually called *breather* or *bleed hole*; one per window, typically centred at the bottom. | [1], [2], [3] |
| 2 | A cabin window is three panes, not one sheet. | ✅ Confirmed for typical airliners (outer pane, middle pane, inner "scratch pane"). Designs vary by aircraft type. | [1], [2], [5] |
| 3 | The pane you can touch is a scratch shield and holds no pressure. | ✅ The inner scratch pane is non-structural and protects the outer two. | [2], [5] |

| 6 | The cabin air is kept much denser. | ✅ Cabin pressure altitude must normally be ≤ 8,000 ft (≈ 75 kPa) → roughly 3× the outside pressure at 35,000 ft. | [6], own calculation |
| 7 | "Hundreds of kilos of force" on every window (on screen: "per window · rough estimate"). | ⚠️ **Own estimate.** Δp ≈ 75.3 − 23.8 ≈ 51 kPa. Exact window sizes and cabin altitude vary, so the video only says "hundreds of kilos". | [6], [7], own calculation |
| 8 | The hole is in the middle pane and lets cabin air into the gap, so the middle pane sees equal pressure on both sides. | ✅ Confirmed. | [1], [2], [3], [4] |
| 9 | The outer pane carries the whole load; the middle pane waits as a backup. | ✅ Confirmed (GKN Aerospace's director of technology, quoted by Gizmodo: pressure is applied "only to the outer pane … preserving the middle pane for emergency situations"). *Simplification:* "unstressed" means it carries almost no pressure load, not literally zero stress. | [2], [3] |
| 10 | The airflow lets moisture escape, so the gap stays clear of fog and frost. | ✅ Confirmed as a second purpose in several sources. *Simplification:* fog/frost depends also on temperature and humidity; the hole helps, it doesn't guarantee a perfectly clear window. | [1], [2], [4] |

## Not claimed on purpose
- No claim that the hole "controls cabin pressure" (it doesn't – the aircraft's pressurisation system does; Boeing notes the small leak through the hole is handled by that system [3]).
- No dramatic failure stories or specific incidents.
