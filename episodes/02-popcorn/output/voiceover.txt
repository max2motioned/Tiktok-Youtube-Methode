Every popcorn kernel is a tiny pressure cooker. And every single one is counting down to an explosion.

Most people think heat just makes the corn burst open. But the real trick is hiding inside.

Each kernel holds a tiny drop of water, about fourteen percent of its weight, packed into soft starch. Around it sits a hard shell that barely lets anything out.

Heat it up, and that water turns into steam. The shell traps it. Pressure climbs to nearly ten times normal air pressure, while the starch inside softens into a hot gel.

At around one hundred eighty degrees Celsius, the shell finally gives up.

In a split second, the steam rushes out and puffs the gel into a foam, up to forty times bigger. It cools almost instantly and sets into that crunchy white cloud.

And the pop you hear? Scientists filmed it at thousands of frames per second. The sound comes from the steam escaping, not from the shell cracking. Even the jump is wild: a little leg of starch kicks the kernel into the air.

The kernels that never pop? Usually a damaged or dried-out shell. The steam leaks away, so the pressure never builds.

One drop of water, one tough shell, one tiny explosion. What everyday thing should we unpack next?
