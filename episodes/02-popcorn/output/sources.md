## Claims in the voiceover

| # | Claim (as spoken / shown) | Status | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A popcorn kernel works like a tiny pressure cooker. | ✅ Common, well-supported analogy: moisture turns to steam, the hard hull (pericarp) holds it in. | [3], [4], [5] |
| 2 | Each kernel holds water, about 14 % of its weight, in soft starch. | ✅ Ideal moisture ≈ 13.5–14 % (Popcorn Board), 13–14.5 % (Purdue, B. Hamaker). On screen: "≈ 14 %". | [4], [5] |
| 3 | A hard shell that barely lets anything out. | ✅ The pericarp is comparatively impermeable; in other grains steam escapes through the hull. *Simplification:* "barely" = slowly, not perfectly sealed. | [3], [5], [6] |
| 4 | Water turns into steam; the shell traps it. | ✅ | [1], [3], [4] |
| 5 | Pressure climbs to nearly ten times normal air pressure. | ✅ Sources: ≈ 135 psi ≈ 9.2 atm [3]; Virot & Ponomarenko estimate ≈ 10 bar ≈ 9.9 atm [1]. "Nearly ten times" covers both. | [1], [3] |
| 6 | The starch softens into a hot gel. | ✅ Starch gelatinises under heat and steam. *Simplification* of a more complex process. | [3], [4] |
| 7 | The shell gives way at around 180 °C. | ✅ Critical temperature ≈ 180 °C, independent of grain size/shape [1]. | [1], [2] |
| 8 | Steam rushes out and puffs the gel into a foam, up to forty times bigger; it sets almost instantly. | ✅ / ⚠️ Expansion is often cited as 40–50× (rule of thumb); Wikipedia gives 20–50×. Industry measures cm³/g instead of a multiplier. The video says "up to forty times", which stays within the cited range. | [3], [7], [8] |
| 9 | Scientists filmed it at thousands of frames per second (on screen "≈ 2,900 frames/sec"). | ✅ High-speed video at 2,900 fps in the 2015 study. | [1], [2] |
| 10 | The pop sound comes from the steam escaping, not from the shell cracking. | ✅ The study links the sound to the release of water vapour; the sound does not coincide with the first split of the hull. *Note:* this is the authors' conclusion from synchronised video/audio. | [1], [2] |
| 11 | A little "leg" of starch kicks the kernel into the air. | ✅ The paper describes a starch "leg" that makes the kernel jump (mm to several cm). | [1], [2] |
| 12 | Kernels that never pop usually have a damaged or dried-out shell; steam leaks, pressure never builds. | ✅ Too little moisture or a damaged/leaky pericarp are the established causes. *Simplification:* hull structure (cellulose order) also matters [6]. | [5], [6], [9] |

## Visual simplifications
- Kernel, cut-away and popcorn are stylised, not to scale; water droplets are symbolic (in reality the moisture is spread through the starch, not in visible drops).
- The pressure gauge is illustrative (≈ 10×), not a measurement.
- The film strip shows a symbolic sequence, not frames from the study.
