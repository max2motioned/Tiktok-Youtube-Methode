Every airplane window has a tiny hole in it. You've probably sat right next to one and never noticed. It's not a crack, and it's not a defect. It's there on purpose, and it quietly does two jobs.

Most people assume a plane window is one thick sheet. It's actually three.

The pane you can touch is just a scratch shield. It holds back no pressure at all. Behind it sit two strong acrylic panes: a middle one and an outer one.

At cruising altitude, the air outside is thin. The air inside is kept much denser, so it pushes outward on every window with hundreds of kilos of force.

That tiny hole goes through the middle pane. Cabin air flows into the gap behind it, so the middle pane feels equal pressure on both sides. The outer pane carries the whole load, and the middle pane waits, unstressed, as a backup.

Job number two: that same airflow lets moisture escape, so the gap stays clear of fog and frost.

One tiny hole. A spare window and a defogger. What everyday thing should we unpack next?
